# A narrow theory of HAL's offline/closed-loop gap

## Main findings

Validation NLL is not a return-ranking statistic. Under matched generative assumptions, however, expert-state log loss can give a generic return bound without learner-occupancy coverage. The performance-difference identity explains why that bound is often loose and which hidden terms matter:

\[
J(\pi)-J(\pi_E)=\sum_{t=1}^T
\mathbb E_{s\sim d_\pi^t,\,a\sim\pi}[A_{\pi_E}^t(s,a)].
\]

Return weights errors by the learner's occupancy, \(d_\pi\), and by expert-continuation advantage, \(A_{\pi_E}\). HAL's ordinary validation loss instead weights log loss by replay occupancy. A diagnostic that samples a model prefix but scores the original expert suffix changes the conditioning distribution without changing that suffix; it is not a proper log score for either complete trajectory distribution ([Huszár](https://arxiv.org/abs/1511.05101)). Its world state also remains a replay state. It does not measure closed-loop occupancy shift.

[Ross, Gordon, and Bagnell](https://proceedings.mlr.press/v15/ross11a.html) show that expert-state 0-1 action error \(\epsilon\) permits a tight \(O(T^2\epsilon)\) gap. Under learner occupancy, if every wrong action increases expert cost-to-go by at most \(u\), the gap is at most \(uT\epsilon\). Stability-constrained imitation refines this with incremental gain stability ([Tu et al.](https://proceedings.mlr.press/v168/tu22a.html)). Ordinary stability is insufficient: contracting systems can amplify expert-distribution error exponentially without broad demonstrations or a suitable policy class ([Simchowitz, Pfrommer, and Jadbabaie](https://proceedings.mlr.press/v291/simchowitz25a.html)).

There is also a direct trajectory argument. If expert and learner use the same initial-state, transition, and opponent kernel, and the policy input is adequate, the chain rule gives

\[
\mathrm{KL}(P_E\|P_\pi)=\sum_t \mathbb E_{s\sim d_E^t}
\mathrm{KL}(\pi_E(\cdot\mid s)\|\pi(\cdot\mid s)).
\]

If total return lies in an interval of width \(R\), Pinsker then gives \(|J(\pi_E)-J(\pi)|\le R\sqrt{\mathrm{KL}(P_E\|P_\pi)/2}\). This is a real expert-state-loss bound. It is commonly vacuous at long horizons because trajectory KL sums over time, and it fails when replay and deployment kernels or information differ. It still does not rank checkpoints: intersecting upper bounds do not order the unknown returns. [Foster, Block, and Misra](https://proceedings.neurips.cc/paper_files/paper/2024/file/da84e39ae51fd26bb5110d9659c06e13-Paper-Conference.pdf) go further: log-loss BC can have horizon-independent sample complexity when the model is realizable, the total-payoff range is controlled, and the supervised complexity of the policy class is controlled. Thus, the generic “BC must compound quadratically” story is too strong.

Coverage and recoverability give a different, sometimes sharper route. If \(C=\max_t\|d_\pi^t/d_E^t\|_\infty<\infty\) and every error changes expert cost-to-go by at most \(u\), one obtains a bound of the form \(uTC\epsilon_E\). This route is useful only if \(C\) and \(u\) are known or controlled, but it localizes the failure modes that an aggregate trajectory bound hides.

For a deterministic expert label, a hypothetical argmax diagnostic obeys \(1[\hat a\ne a_E]\le -\log \pi(a_E\mid h)/\log 2\). This can connect diagnostic NLL to the loose bound above, but O50's live controller uses seeded categorical sampling, not argmax. For a stochastic or heterogeneous demonstrator, only *excess* cross-entropy is a KL divergence, and the irreducible conditional entropy remains. Pinsker can then bound total variation and, with bounded action advantage, the expected advantage gap. It does not bound disagreement between independent expert and policy samples, nor the behavior of an argmax policy without further margin assumptions. In neither case does one upper bound rank two policies. Even fixed occupancy and advantage bounds are insufficient for ranking: scalar NLL needs additional order-preserving structure that these bounds do not supply. The cleanest scalar would estimate the performance-difference integrand itself. HAL's return-based AWR weights are useful, but logged trajectory return is not the counterfactual \(A_{\pi_E}(s,a)\); it must not be described as such.

## Why lower NLL or more data can hurt

Five mechanisms fit this system.

1. **Rare transitions dominate play.** Holding a controller input is common. A model can reduce average NLL by becoming a better copy machine while becoming worse at the rare release, reversal, recovery, or defensive transition that changes a stock. This is the established copycat form of causal confusion ([de Haan et al.](https://proceedings.neurips.cc/paper/2019/hash/947018640bf36a2bb609d3557a285329-Abstract.html); [Wen et al.](https://proceedings.neurips.cc/paper/2020/hash/1b113258af3968aaf3969ca67e744ff8-Abstract.html)). HAL already logs transition accuracy and change F1; these should be split by gameplay consequence and learner occupancy.

2. **Teacher-forced history can carry latent intent.** Replays are not generated by a policy conditioned only on the recorded observables. Player intent persists across time. Conditional BC averages over that latent variable. During training, prior expert actions reveal it; during play, prior model actions can create a self-confirming but wrong intent. Re-querying a multimodal conditional each frame can also switch modes, while a joint action chunk commits to one coherent sample. This is consistent with Seo's controlled observations that history can lower offline error but worsen control and that action chunks can outperform one-step replanning ([Behavioral cloning mystery](https://seohong.me/blog/behavioral-cloning-mystery/)). Multimodal action data are a known BC problem ([Behavior Transformer](https://arxiv.org/abs/2206.11251)).

3. **More data may mean a different policy.** Adding players, skill tiers, or situations changes \(p_D(a\mid h)\). Its Bayes predictor can be less coherent for any one matchup even while aggregate NLL improves. A larger model can exploit identity, previous-action, or matchup correlates more precisely. If data are instead nested IID samples from one fixed distribution, the population optimum is unchanged: systematic harm then points to finite compute, optimization/implicit bias, or closed-loop selection, not a mystical anti-scaling law.

4. **The loss decomposes where the game does not.** HAL sums autoregressive group and horizon NLLs. That is a valid joint log-likelihood for the modeled factorization, but gameplay cost is not additive across stick, trigger, button, and time. A tiny probability change can lower NLL without changing argmax; a one-bin argmax change can cross a collision or ledge boundary.

5. **Minibatch noise can move return without moving NLL.** [Block et al.](https://proceedings.iclr.cc/paper_files/paper/2024/hash/f8b06ac8601473c3232aec9facbe79ad-Abstract-Conference.html) observe sharp long-horizon reward oscillations under negligibly changing BC loss and trace them to SGD noise amplified by unstable closed-loop dynamics. EMA of iterates helped in their tasks. This is direct prior art for a low-NLL/unstable-return checkpoint trace, but not evidence that HAL has the same mechanism, that Muon is uniquely responsible, or that EMA will help Melee. It predicts that adjacent-checkpoint output perturbations and reward variance should co-move even when validation NLL does not.

## Muon versus AdamW

Muon discards the singular values of its momentum matrix and approximately equalizes update singular values. HAL then applies a shape scale. Near a supervised optimum, the raw gradient can shrink while the normalized matrix update does not; the learning-rate schedule, momentum coherence, and weight norm determine the actual functional step.

[Hyperball](https://arxiv.org/abs/2606.16899) formalizes a useful diagnostic: for scale-invariant matrix blocks, weight decay sets an equilibrium radius and thereby an angular learning rate, approximately the update norm divided by the weight norm. It also shows that Muon and AdamW have different update norms and autocorrelations. But its theorem assumes scale invariance, stationary idealized gradients, constant hyperparameters, and exact SVD Muon. HAL has residual paths, mixed Muon/AdamW parameters, a cosine schedule, and five-step Newton--Schulz. The implementation itself says its output singular values are only approximately in \([0.5,1.5]\). Measure the claimed geometry; do not import the theorem as an explanation. Recent Muon work independently reports spectral-norm drift driven by row magnitudes and sensitivity to weight decay ([Muown](https://arxiv.org/abs/2605.10797)).

Raw Hessian top eigenvalue is a weak arbiter here. Hessian sharpness changes under function-preserving reparameterization ([Dinh et al.](https://proceedings.mlr.press/v70/dinh17b.html)), and Muon's relevant step geometry is not Euclidean. A Hessian-vector product can diagnose a fixed checkpoint and parameterization, but optimizer-matched loss response and output change are better evidence. Compare actual matrix angle, radial change, singular spectrum, and the change in validation logits or KL caused by one replayed update.

The closest primary result to HAL's nearly hard Muon attention is [Zhai et al.'s attention entropy collapse](https://proceedings.mlr.press/v202/zhai23a.html). They average row entropy over all query positions, heads, and examples; low entropy accompanies loss oscillation or divergence across several Transformer tasks. A temperature intervention early in ViT training lowers entropy, raises their parameter-Hessian eigenvalue, and can cause instability, while the same intervention later can recover. Their spectral argument gives an input-dependent entropy lower bound that can fall exponentially as the operator norm of the QK logit map grows. HAL's result is compatible but not equivalent: we measured only the final query on fixed 256-frame contexts, Q/K Frobenius norms rather than the QK operator norm, and no Hessian or update instability at those exact checkpoints. Muon attention stays near one-hot through gameplay recovery, and restoring early Q/K norms only partly softens it with uncertain small NLL harm. The historical comparison fixes architecture and representation, but its learning-rate recipes and training trajectories differ. It is not an optimizer-only causal estimate. Thus low entropy is an optimizer-associated representation marker and a causal-test target, not evidence by itself of training instability, curvature, or bad retrieval. Zhai et al. explicitly list the causal relation between entropy collapse and instability as unresolved.

## Attractors, chaos, and circuits

A likely related paper is [*Fractal basins trap latent reasoning*](https://arxiv.org/abs/2609.04963), but the tweet text was unavailable, so this attribution is not verified. The paper varies initial latent states of recurrent-depth solvers. It finds a training bifurcation where incorrect fixed points become saddles, finite-time Lyapunov exponents become positive, and settling-time basins become fractal. HAL's transformer does not iterate a latent state to a fixed point. The valid analogue is the deterministic composite map consisting of policy, 256-frame history update, opponent, and Dolphin. A positive short-horizon separation rate in this hybrid system means sensitivity; it is not by itself evidence of a strange attractor or chaos. Collision boundaries can produce the same observation.

For circuits, begin with a behavioral contrast, not an attention heat map. Transformer heads move information between residual-stream positions through separable QK and OV operations ([Elhage et al.](https://transformer-circuits.pub/2021/framework/index.html)), but attention weights alone need not explain outputs ([Jain and Wallace](https://aclanthology.org/N19-1357/)). Use matched clean/corrupt inputs and causal activation or head-output patching; results depend strongly on the corruption and metric ([Zhang and Nanda](https://proceedings.iclr.cc/paper_files/paper/2024/hash/06a52a54c8ee03cd86771136bc91eb1f-Abstract-Conference.html)). The first concrete contrast should separate “repeat my last action” from “change because the world changed.”

## Four cheap, falsifiable checkpoint tests

1. **Occupancy–advantage error table.** On the same saved start states, collect short deterministic rollouts for several checkpoints. Bin frames by replay versus learner occupancy, action transition type, and a fixed consequence proxy such as subsequent damage/stock swing. Report NLL, exact-frame error, and change F1 in every bin. Treatment is checkpoint; starts, opponent, seeds, and horizon are invariant. If aggregate NLL improves while learner-occupancy error or consequential-transition error worsens, the offline gap is explained without mech-interp. The consequence proxy is not action advantage; label it accordingly.

2. **Copy-versus-state causal patch.** Build real matched prefix pairs with the same last action but different imminent expert transitions, and the converse. Patch action-history tokens, world-state tokens, then each head output between pair members. Measure the target logit margin and full four-frame argmax sequence. Include self-patches, label-preserving pairs, both patch directions, and head ablations. Synthetic action-only corruption is an off-manifold confound, so use it only after the real-pair result. Causal confusion and patching are prior art; comparing the causal profile across success and late-failure checkpoints is a HAL-specific combination, not an established novelty claim.

3. **Local closed-loop separation map.** From exact Dolphin savestates near neutral, recovery, ledge, and hit-confirm situations, run identical replicas and replicas with one minimal legal input or state perturbation. Use common randomness. Plot feature distance, first action divergence, and outcome versus time; estimate a finite-time separation rate and a small 2-D outcome/settling-time basin. Identical-replica divergence is the determinism control. Repeat away from collision thresholds and across coordinate scalings. A late-checkpoint increase predicts fragile closed-loop play; a fractal-looking picture alone proves little.

4. **Optimizer-geometry replay.** Without mutating the run, restore each checkpoint and optimizer state, compute one fixed minibatch update, and log per matrix: \(\|W\|_F\), \(\|W\|_{op}\), stable rank, row-norm spread, radial update, actual angle, update singular values, and momentum autocorrelation. Apply scaled copies of the update to a disposable model and measure loss and output KL/Jensen--Shannon change. Separate Muon matrices from AdamW embeddings and heads. Match Muon and AdamW by output change, not nominal learning rate. This tests the predicted functional-step change under an angular/norm-drift explanation. A single minibatch is a diagnostic, not a complete test of training stability.

These tests follow the experimental advice common to [Schulman's guide](http://joschu.net/blog/opinionated-guide-ml-research.html) and [Karpathy's recipe](https://karpathy.github.io/2019/04/25/recipe/): isolate one hypothesis, make its expected observation explicit, and prefer a small diagnostic that can kill it. None requires a new training run.
